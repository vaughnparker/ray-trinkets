The Spike Ball Riddle

A math riddle about rotational symmetry.

The riddle

A spike ball is a ball with a finite number of spikes sticking straight out from its center, all the same length. Two spike balls are the same design if you can turn one so that its spikes land exactly where the other's are.

A turn rotates the whole ball about a line through its center. A turn matches the ball to itself if, after it, every spike sits where some spike sat before.

Which spike balls obey all three rules?

  1. Not on one circle. The spike tips don't all lie on a single circle.
  2. The same from every spike. For any two spikes, some turn of the ball moves the first spike to where the second one was, and matches the ball to itself.
  3. Every spike is a pivot. For every spike, some turn about that spike's own line, by more than 0° and less than a full 360°, matches the ball to itself.

How many different designs are there?

The setup

The same design

Only the pattern of the spikes matters, not which way the ball happens to be facing. Turn the ball and you still have the same design. The two balls below are the same design: one turn takes the left one onto the right one.

You can't flip a ball to its mirror image, and you can't make the spikes longer or shorter. Spikes always point straight out from the center.

Rule 1

Not on one circle

Look at the tip of every spike. Rule 1 says the tips can't all sit on one circle. The circle can be any size, and it doesn't have to go around the middle of the ball.

Any three tips lie on a circle, so a ball with three spikes or fewer always breaks this rule. That keeps out the boring cases. Rule 1 on its own is easy to pass.

Rule 2

The same from every spike

Hold the ball by one spike and look at it. Now hold it by a different spike. Rule 2 says you get the same view both times. That means there's a turn that carries the first spike to where the second one was, with all the spikes landing on spikes again.

The ring on the left breaks rule 1, but it's here to show rule 2 on its own.

Rule 3

Every spike is a pivot

Pick a spike. The line through it and the center of the ball is its axis. Turning the ball about that axis leaves the spike itself in place. Rule 3 says that for every spike, some turn about its axis, by less than a full turn, matches the ball to itself.

Turning by 0° or by a full 360° matches any ball to itself, so those don't count. Drag the sliders to look for an angle in between.

The ring on the left breaks rule 1, and the ball on the right breaks rule 3 for every one of its spikes. They are here to show rule 3 on its own.
