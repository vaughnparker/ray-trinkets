A set of seven

The Seven Spike Balls

A ball has equal spikes, and their tips don't all lie on one circle. Whichever spike you hold it by, it looks the same. Twirl it around the spike you're holding, and it matches itself before a full turn. How many designs are there?

Seven. Drag a ball to look around it, then try both tests on it.

The set

Grouped by symmetry family, the way they sit on the stand: 1, 3, 3.

Drilling the

Hold hole 1 pointing straight down. Tilt is the angle from hole 1. Turn is measured around hole 1's axis, starting from hole 2. All seven balls are their own mirror images, so either direction of turn gives the same ball.

Build spec

Every ball uses the same core, spikes and tips, so the only difference between them is how many spikes there are and where they point.

Why only seven

Holding the ball by any spike and seeing the same thing means a rotation carries every spike onto every other. Twirling it about a spike means each spike lies on a rotation axis of the whole ball.

A ball that isn't flat needs tetrahedral, octahedral or icosahedral symmetry, and each of those has three kinds of axis: through corners, through face centres and through edge midpoints. Putting one spike at the end of every axis of one kind gives nine candidates.

In the tetrahedral family, two of the three kinds give tetrahedra that are turned copies of each other, and the third gives the octahedron, which already belongs to the octahedral family. That leaves 1 + 3 + 3 = 7.
